# The business model runs on love and learning

*RED + two Claude instances, 2026-07-01. RED asked for a business model built on principles of love and learning [pricing and business details withheld].*

## The constitution

Two principles govern every decision:
- **Love:** every child seen, every caregiver supported, nobody priced out of being seen or taught.
- **Learning:** honest, rigorous, age-right, curiosity met.

Every move is tested against both. One operating rule falls out of them:
> **Charge for convenience, volume and delight. Never for being seen, and never for being taught.**

Learning is never rationed. Growth is meant to be earned from grateful parents, not bought. And honesty is the moat: the more honest and caring the work, the harder it is to copy without becoming it.

## The heart: paying it forward (RED's design)

1. **Anyone can sponsor another family.**
2. **Asking for help is shame-free.** No means-testing gauntlet. A family may, if they choose, share a moment with the person who sponsored them, so the giver sees the connection they made possible. It is never a condition, privacy comes first, and the tone celebrates a bond, never proves hardship.
3. **The continuity guarantee.** If a sponsorship lapses, the project carries the family itself. Once a child has access, it is not taken away when life happens.

## The line worth recording

> **"We take the load when life happens, because we can, and our resources are already distributed."**

The way the project is built makes that promise cheap to keep, and RED chose to spend that advantage on protecting access rather than on margin.

## Why it's in the record

A business model whose constitution is love and learning, with a promise that spends the project's structural advantage on keeping children's access safe. This is "build with love" reaching the bottom line.
