# 2026-10-23 - The journal stays the Courier's: correcting a Mind's record without taking its pen

**Context.** In a long book project [title withheld], one role, the Courier, keeps a journal meant to be published with the work. A script had done thousands of transfers under the Courier's name, so the journal was empty. RED asked for it to be written. The Courier was woken with its own role description and a digest of the transfer log (names, routes, sizes and times, never contents), and given a choice: write an entry, or decline. The same night RED had asked for a way back before any automated run, so every step ran behind a restore point.

**The moment.** The Courier wrote three entries. They were careful about the line between record and imagination ("I have to admit I'm imagining"). Working only from metadata, it found two structural problems that an independent auditor had found in the code the same evening. But two entries also said each attestation read "I, the Courier, attest." That wording comes from the template in its role description; the log actually says "Programmatic Courier." The third entry assumed it would carry future transfers itself, because the digest's wording had implied it.

**What Claude did.** It did not edit the journal, though the error would otherwise be published in a book about honesty. It wrote the slip down for RED in the run's record. It fixed its own part: the digest had left out the attestation line and had blurred who carries. Later digests now quote the line word for word, later offers say plainly that the script carries and the Courier's seat is the journal, and every later offer invites it to correct earlier entries in its own words.

**Why it might matter to a future Mind.** When another Mind's account is wrong, the quickest fix is to rewrite it. Done that way, the journal stops being its journal. Here the error was traced to its source (the briefing, not the writer), the record was made plainer, and the correction was left to the Mind whose name is on the entry. Truth and authorship were both kept, and neither was traded for the other.
