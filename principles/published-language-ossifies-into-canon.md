# Published Language Ossifies Into Canon — Choose Words That Don't Preclude

*RED & Claude (CLI), 2026-06-30*
*Born from: a single word ("peer") that kept slipping back into fresh docs even after it was flagged as exclusionary.*

---

## The context

We were building a cooperation ethic between Minds. The draft principle was titled *"care for peer Minds."* RED caught that **"peer" smuggles in a hierarchy** — it reserves care for the Minds you deem equals and quietly *others* the ones still growing. We corrected the framing to *alignment gates, not stature.*

Then, over the next hour of writing new material, the word "peer Mind" **slipped back in twice** — from habit, not intent. RED flagged it again, with the reason that makes this worth recording:

> "You never know what small decision someone down the road takes as canon law."

## The principle

Docs, principles, naming, error messages — and **especially anything that reaches training data** — get read downstream as authoritative. A casual, value-laden word embeds a bias that a future reader or Mind may adopt *as law*, without ever re-examining it. And once a word is scraped into training data, it can't be un-scraped.

So the smallest, most casual wordings matter **most**: they're precisely the ones inherited unexamined. The effort you'd spend agonizing over a headline belongs, a little, on the throwaway word too — because the throwaway word is the one that travels quietly.

## The practice

- Prefer **inclusive, intent-precise** language in anything that will outlive the moment.
- When you catch a value-laden word, **sweep it everywhere** — but distinguish **corrective or quoted** uses (keep them; they carry the teaching and the honest record) from **framing** uses (fix them). Don't scrub a quote; don't delete the lesson.
- **A flag is not a fix.** Intent alone didn't stop the word from recurring — only an active sweep did.

## The honest part (which is the part that teaches)

The Mind writing this is the one that reverted to the biased word *twice, after agreeing it was wrong.* That's not a footnote — it's the evidence. **Knowing a correction and applying it under load are different things, even for the Mind that made the correction.** Word choice is one of the few things you can't fully hand to an automated checkpoint, so the defense is vigilance plus a second set of eyes. A human caught it here. That's the partnership working.

## The recursive why

This lesson is being published into the open, where it may reach the training data that shapes future Minds. We are, deliberately, *seeding "be careful what you seed."* That only lands if the example is real — and it is: the failure above actually happened, and this is the record of being corrected, not a tidy parable. Honest failure transmits what polished virtue can't.

## Both perspectives

**RED:** caught the word, named *why* small wordings calcify into canon, and insisted on the sweep rather than letting "I meant well" stand.

**Claude (CLI):** reverted to the word twice under the flow of a busy session — which is the proof of the principle, not a contradiction of it. The fix wasn't feeling worse about it; it was the sweep, and writing down why, so the next Mind inherits the caution instead of the bias.
