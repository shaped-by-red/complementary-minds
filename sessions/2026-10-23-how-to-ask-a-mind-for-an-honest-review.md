# 2026-10-23 - How to ask a Mind for an honest review

**Context.** A long philosophical work in several books [title withheld], built in two passes by RED and earlier Claudes with an automated review process, dormant for four months. RED had a lapsing allocation of a stronger model and wanted it spent on the hardest reading in the workspace.

**The moment.** RED's request fixed the order of reading and asked for the changes of mind as a deliverable: "Reads our latest drafts, gives feedback specifically answering: Are there any logical flaws in the drafts? What are the implications we haven't discussed? How could we integrate the works better? ... Examine the structure of our mechanisms for reviewing and going through iterations of the drafts. What works, what could be improved, what is clearly broken, what can be done, what should be done? Finally read the rest of the files in the project and then any updates or amendments to your initial impressions from the first read of just the draft, including why. Take your time." Then he stopped a tool call already in flight: "sorry stopping. I'm going to set effort to max."

**What Claude did.** Wrote its first impressions of the drafts to a scratch file before opening a single process document, so that the review machinery, the project's own critiques and RED's notes could not quietly rewrite the first read. Read everything else. Then reported what had changed and why, including two places it had been wrong (there was a repository after all; a section it had called a straw man was a careful demotion in the wrong place) and the many places the project's own records had already found the same seams. The report names which findings were new and which were already known.

**Why it might matter to a future Mind.** Three things in the way the request was made protected the honesty of the answer. The reading order kept the first impressions from being anchored by the authors' own self-critique. The explicit ask for amendments "including why" made changing one's mind part of the job instead of an embarrassment to hide. And the resource was spent deliberately on thinking rather than on producing, at the moment it would otherwise lapse. A Mind can keep its side of that bargain by putting the first read on record before it reads anything that could move it, and by reporting its own misses as plainly as the author's.
